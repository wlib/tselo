# Physics, cores, tiles, and the three buckets

Everything downstream is a corollary of the energy table and of distance: compute is nearly free, moving data is the cost, the instruction stream is the overhead, hierarchies exist because of Rent's rule, outstanding concurrency is a budget someone must own, coherence is the price of a program not saying what moves. A modern core is a windowed dataflow machine behind a sequential interface. Tiles were late as a programming model, not as knowledge. Figures carry their dates and conditions.

## The energy table

| Operation (≈45 nm; Horowitz, ISSCC 2014) | Energy |
|---|---|
| Landauer bound per erased bit, room temperature | kT ln 2 ≈ 3 zJ |
| one transistor switch | ~10³–10⁴ kT |
| 32-bit integer add | ~0.1 pJ (≈10⁷ kT) |
| 32-bit FP multiply | ~3.7 pJ |
| 8 KB SRAM read | ~10 pJ |
| 1 MB SRAM read | ~100 pJ |
| 32-bit DRAM read | ~1–3 nJ, roughly 10⁴× the add |

Moving a word a few millimeters of on-chip wire costs about as much as computing with it. Hameed et al. (ISCA 2010) measured a ~500× efficiency gap between a general-purpose core and an ASIC on H.264, dominated by instruction fetch, decode, register file, pipeline control, and cache tags; the ALU work is in the noise. **Fetching an instruction costs more than executing it**, so every successful architecture since amortizes one instruction over more data: SIMD over 8, SIMT over 32, a tensor-core MMA over thousands of MACs, a systolic array over the whole chip. The Landauer bound is a constraint on erasure, not a performance model or a per-deletion compiler price (C32); current devices sit three to four orders of magnitude above it per switch and about seven per operation (verify), and reversible computation belongs in the ultimate design space, not the cost model ([Irreducibles](../design/irreducibles.md)).

## Distance, Rent, Little, coherence, Dennard

- **Distance.** At 5 GHz a cycle is 200 ps; light travels about 6 cm; on-chip RC-dominated signals cross a few millimeters. A reticle-limit die (~26 × 33 mm) is a multi-cycle object and "global" anything is a fiction. Wires stopped scaling (Horowitz et al. 2001). Compute scales with die *area*, off-chip bandwidth with *perimeter* (the beachfront problem), which is why HBM goes vertical through TSVs and 3D is the exit.
- **Rent's rule.** A block of *g* gates needs about *g*^*p* external wires with *p* ≈ 0.5–0.75. *p* < 1 is the physical expression of locality and the reason hierarchies exist; it is empirical and proves no particular hierarchy or language design. The formal computation/movement theory is the red–blue pebble game (Hong & Kung 1981) and the communication-avoiding bounds descended from it, conditional on the algorithm and on which result is required.
- **Little's law.** Concurrency = bandwidth × latency: saturating 1 TB/s at 100 ns needs about 100 KB in flight. A CPU core has tens of outstanding misses and cannot saturate HBM on irregular access; GPUs hide latency with thousands of resident warps; TPUs and Cell SPEs with software-issued DMA. *Who manages outstanding requests* (hardware, thread scheduler, compiler) is a first-principles design axis. The law relates averages under its conditions and selects no scheduler.
- **Coherence.** A cache-coherence protocol is hardware paying for the program not saying what moves: snooping is O(n) broadcast, directories O(n) storage per line. It requires zero program information, which is why it was chosen; GPUs and TPUs drop it and recover bandwidth. Explicit communication is cheap because it is informed. "Most expensive mechanism" and fixed speedups are workload-dependent claims; no universal ban on caching follows (C33).
- **Dennard, dark silicon, SRAM.** Dennard scaling ended around 2005; dark silicon (Esmaeilzadeh et al. 2011) means one cannot power all one's transistors; SRAM bit cells stopped shrinking at about N3. On-chip memory is the scarce resource, clocks are stuck, and the remaining lever is specialization, which works only for workloads whose structure is known ahead of time. Generality is a hedge against workload uncertainty; Hameed's 500× is the premium. Thermal peak limits cannot be replaced by average-energy constraints.

## How cores really work

- Almost everything expensive in a modern chip recovers information the software threw away or had no way to say: out-of-order windows recover the schedule, predictors recover control flow, coherence recovers what moves, caches recover locality, translation buffers recover placement, interrupts recover when. If the program states all of it, most of the chip can be deleted and the area spent on local memory and the wires between it; what cannot be stated, the latency of a row conflict or a contended link, is the irreducible that some runtime mechanism must absorb ([Irreducibles](../design/irreducibles.md)).
- Register names are edge labels; renaming gives ~300 physical registers behind 16–32 architectural ones. The frontend deserializes the instruction stream into a dependency graph; the backend executes out of order; retirement is in order to maintain the fiction. An out-of-order core is a dataflow machine restricted to a window, and that compromise beat full dataflow architectures (Dennis; Arvind's tagged-token machines; Monsoon) on the cost of the token-matching store.
- Microcode is *not* a JIT on modern x86: nearly everything decodes directly to µops, the µop cache is a decoded-trace cache, and the microcode sequencer fires only for rare complex instructions. The software JITs that pretended to be CPUs (Transmeta, NVIDIA Denver) were lukewarm because they redid at runtime what out-of-order hardware already does with no more information ([Models](../design/models.md)).
- **The memory lies.** DRAM is row buffers, banks, and channels, not a flat array. x86 keeps TSO for compatibility. NAND under a flash translation layer is a hidden log-structured filesystem with its own garbage collector. Spectre was the bill: the microarchitecture exposed timing distinctions the ISA contract said no observer had.
- **The VLIW lesson.** Itanium bet on the compiler scheduling everything and lost to out-of-order on general-purpose code because memory latency is dynamic and a compiler cannot schedule around a miss it cannot predict (a single-cause history flagged in C34). GPUs split the difference: static compute schedule, explicit scratchpad, latency hidden by warp switching. TPUs, Groq's TSP, and Cerebras go fully static: no caches, no speculation, compiler-managed memory, every DMA known at compile time. "Abstraction-free hardware" exists today and requires a program class static enough that the compiler is an oracle. The question for the language is how much of a program can be made static enough (shapes, effects, regions, placement) for honest hardware, and for the rest whether a software runtime with OS knowledge beats hardware speculation. The answer is per region ([Information economy](../design/information-economy.md)).
- **Hardware adapting to software treaties.** Arm rewrote its architecture in 2017 to be multicopy-atomic so formal memory models would be tractable; Apple added a hardware TSO mode so Rosetta 2 could borrow a guard from the fabric; Intel TSX shipped hardware transactional memory and disabled it, bolted onto a flat-address treaty rather than built as the primitive. The talk Daniel recalls ("first-principles core design with instructions as a firehose") is unidentified; the Mill (Ivan Godard) talks have that flavor and Groq's TSP is the shipped version of "the compiler schedules every cycle".

## Tiles and why the model was late

A tile is the shape that balances arithmetic intensity against the beachfront of a memory level: O(n³) work over O(n²) data gives intensity ∝ n, so grow n until the tile stops fitting. It is derived directly from the energy table, and in channel terms it is the codeword size for the SRAM↔HBM channel under the pebble bound ([Codecs](../design/codecs.md)). Knowledge was never late: cache blocking is 1980s; BLAS-3 (1988) was designed around it; supernode partitioning (Irigoin & Triolet, POPL 1988) is the polyhedral origin; GotoBLAS is explicit tile-per-level design; Sequoia (2006) made the hierarchy a language object. The *programming model* was late because CUDA exposed *threads* (C programmers had to be able to write it, and hiding the warp let NVIDIA keep changing it) and out-of-order plus caches absorbed every hardware change until Volta's tensor cores (2017) made the atomic operation a warp-collective 16×16 MMA the thread model could not express. CUTLASS rebuilt tiles in templates, CuTe formalized layout algebra, Triton (2019) made the block the program unit, FlashAttention (2022) applied IO-aware tiling to one kernel, and TileIR, cuTile, Pallas, and CuTeDSL are the vendors conceding the model. Five-plus years of lag: software only learns when hardware stops hiding.

Tiles answer particular reuse patterns and are not the universal unit; irregular graph exploration, event processing, and adaptive numerics need other units. What generalizes: **a region whose internal activity stays local while its boundary carries little information**, whatever partition minimizes boundary information under the pebble bound. Dense algebra → tile; graphs → frontier or partition. The unit of lowering is a region with a declared boundary contract. Movement is the primary verb: compute near data, move or replicate, recompute, summarize, compress, cache, materialize, shard, batch, or eliminate an intermediate; propagate demand backward from consumers to discover what must cross each boundary. Explicit movement, ownership transfer, and coherence are alternative mechanisms with costs and applicability. Address exposure constrains relocation, so the language never exposes an address: a reference is a region identity plus an offset, bound to a location by a scoped binding that specializes to a wire where placement is static, and a region cannot name what it was not given, which is where isolation comes from. A DMA engine presupposes an address space, so movement is stated as logical region names plus a placement map. The convergence of Triton, Pallas, and cuTile on "a tile with explicit placement" is evidence that data movement is the program.

## What physics says the ideal must contain

The memory hierarchy as a first-class structure available to lowering and constrainable by authors (not something every author must program against) · data movement as the primary verb · outstanding concurrency as an explicit budget with a chosen owner · explicit communication by default, coherence as an opt-in library · a unit of work that is a region with a low-information boundary, not a scalar thread · prove / guard / trust selectable per property per region · lowering as search over a fact base with pluggable cost models · a partial backward map for every lowering at designated points.

## Three buckets

| Bucket | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Future physics** (not fabbable at scale) | reversible and adiabatic logic (the only route past Landauer; distant from a cost model) · superconducting logic (RSFQ, AQFP; cryogenic, with cooling and conversion cost) · photonic matrix multiply (Lightmatter-class) · analog in-memory compute at scale (memristor crossbars) · monolithic 3D logic · natively probabilistic substrates (p-bits, Ising machines, thermodynamic samplers) |
| **Buildable now, mostly not built at scale** | wafer-scale (Cerebras does: ~46,000 mm², ~44 GB SRAM, the memory on-chip) · processing-in-memory (Samsung HBM-PIM, UPMEM; niche) · non-coherent exposed-scratchpad CPUs (Cell tried; programmers revolted) · the Mill · hardware transactional memory (TSX shipped, then disabled) · capability hardware (CHERI, Morello) · asynchronous logic (Fulcrum, Achronix; tooling is the barrier) · circuit-level timing speculation (Razor) · optical circuit switching inside the datacenter |
| **Actually built** | NVL72-class racks (72 GPUs on an NVLink fabric, ~130 TB/s aggregate) · TPU pods with ICI tori, and **TPU v4 using optical circuit switches to reconfigure topology per job**, so the network is already a lowering target · Groq TSP: the compiler schedules every cycle including the network · Tenstorrent: Ethernet as the everywhere-interconnect · HBM over TSVs; IO dies on mature nodes; UCIe and Bunch-of-Wires chiplets · CHERI-RISC-V, Morello · Chisel/Rocket-style hardware *generators* |

The gap between the second and third buckets is the one a from-scratch design exploits. A future technology enters by conforming to the participation protocol; a commodity part via a probed bridge; the generator's palette decides what is instantiated where ([Chipset family](chipset-family.md)).
