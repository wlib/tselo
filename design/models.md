# Models and controllers

The stack is already a tower of estimators at different time constants with no shared vocabulary. Make the models explicit, with provenance and validity domains, and govern them with controllers at stratified timescales; the problem is adaptive control, not compilation. The **mapper** (a runtime's term for the pluggable policy deciding placement, scheduling, and representation) is the shape of every installable strategy. Data structures, sorting, and precision become derived facts chosen under a workload model with a worst-case bound. Profile-guided optimization is the special case.

## What already runs a model

A branch predictor (TAGE, perceptrons) is an online learner in silicon at nanosecond time constants; an inline cache is a frequency model of shapes at a call site; a JIT's tier-up thresholds are hand-set bandit parameters and its deopt counts an unacknowledged regret signal; Android aggregates profiles across devices and ships them with the APK, while Google's warehouse-scale profiling feeds AutoFDO and Meta's BOLT re-lays-out binaries from production profiles, so the datacenter is already the profiler though nobody closed the loop into one derivation graph; a query optimizer tracks interesting orders across operators and Bao's contextual bandit explores plan hints on a fraction of queries; a NAND controller runs LDPC belief propagation with read-retry on every read; Borg Autopilot uses hysteresis and dwell times; the author choosing a hash map is a model fit once, by hand, never updated ([Systems](../evidence/systems.md)).

## The model DAG

Root: the hardware's self-description ([Participation](participation.md)). Above it a *target model* (energy, latency, bandwidth per level, topology, error curves), an *input model* (profiles, declared priors, property-test samples), a *semantic model* (types, effects, equations: the certain part), and an *intent model* (what the author claims about distributions and wants). Each is derived state with a frontier, a construction cost, a staleness, and the policy under which its data was collected. A lowering is a decision under uncertainty: minimize expected cost under current knowledge subject to the certain constraints, with a worst-case bound beside the expectation. PGO stops being a mode and becomes ordinary updating of the input model.

**Degrees of search are a ladder, and the rung is a decision**: hard-coded default → author choice → heuristic → parametric model (roofline, the energy table) → learned model → online bandit → full synthesis with proof. The rung depends on how much the fragment runs, and content addressing makes the rung climbed a cached fact shared by everyone ([Store](store.md)). The rungs are not strictly ordered: a coarse bound can be more robust than a miscalibrated detailed model, and not every predictor needs a Bayesian implementation. Instrumentation is a value-of-information problem (counters where the posterior is uncertain *and* the decision is sensitive; collecting every counter is not free, Q12), and exploration is explicit (run an alternative lowering on 1% of executions).

## Control, not compilation

- **Observations record their collection policy.** A profile observed while batching is not a profile of unbatched behavior; moving work changes contention, contention changes timing, timing changes user behavior; the optimizer intervenes in the system it learns; off-policy evaluation is the existing tool (Q11). Record workload, policy, versions, placement, competitors, sampling scheme, and target state with every measurement. Mechanisms: counters, sampling, controlled alternative execution where valid, simulation, replay, shadow computation with suppressed effects, hardware telemetry, prototypes, human evaluation.
- **No single universal optimizer.** Controllers cooperate through shared contracts and budgets at separated timescales (predictor in nanoseconds, JIT in milliseconds, fleet model in hours, author's prior in months, standards in years); a circuit-level controller must not wait for a fleet engine, and a fleet optimizer must not read every fast local adjustment as a reason to relocate work. Timescale separation (slow sees fast as instantaneous; fast sees slow's setpoints as constant) is a condition to analyze, not a guarantee; non-stationarity is handled by time constants, not by trusting the freshest estimate; the system must know which model is authoritative at which scale rather than letting the fastest win, and the arbitration rule between a slow and a fast controller is open (Q10).
- **Adaptation is judged over a horizon**: benefit minus search, measurement, migration, and interference cost, with reversal cost, hysteresis, minimum residence times, reservations, and explicit ownership of each decision. The optimizer must be able to conclude that further optimization is a bad use of resources; many controllers relocating work at once can amplify load (Q14).
- **Universal meanings, plural controllers.** Compatible meanings and exchange protocols, not one physical store or access to the same facts; which knowledge is materialized where is itself a placement problem ([Change](change.md)).
- **Causality.** A global model can describe states no participant jointly observes; a valid policy chooses actions from the information its actor has at that point (a receiver cannot use an undecoded value; a fleet controller cannot assume instantaneous visibility; a future measurement cannot justify an earlier irreversible action). Logical order, physical time, wall-clock estimates, and version identifiers are different things related by explicit models. Experiments, instrumentation, simulation, and human consultation are candidate *actions* within the design process ([Codecs](codecs.md)); phrasing a task as a partially observed stochastic process does not supply a solver.
- **Author-declared priors** are inputs with provenance, never silently overridden by measurement without the author able to see why ([Store](store.md)).

Two unifications with the article: coupled decisions are a glitch-freedom problem (two derivations of one workload model updated out of step give a correctness bug born of a stale frontier, so envelopes bound which decisions must share a model version), and model drift is invalidation (a measured distribution contradicting a declared prior is a predicate firing on a derived decision, today a silent regression; every decision carries provenance and the debugger shows it). Open on profiling and control: causal and off-policy evaluation, correlated observations, cold start, stability across timescales, privacy of shared profiles, and a reproducible way to compare adaptive policies including transition overhead (Q48).

## Data structures as codecs

Daniel's requirement: nobody should implement a binary search, a skip list, or a Bloom filter, or decide where to sort. Authors declare operations, invariants, tolerances, and optionally beliefs about the distribution; the structure, the sort, the index, and the precision are derived facts with provenance. The price is the declaration: for the system to have freedom the author must say what is observable (the observer checklist in [Observers](observers.md)), and that is harder than naming a hash map. Ownership in Rust is evidence that authors accept such declarations when the payoff is visible; it remains a usability bet, measured by open boundary surface, since inside closed regions the checklist is inferred and only open boundaries need declarations ([Observers](observers.md), Q58). The framing: a data structure is a codec for the key→position access channel under a source model, with functional compression when the consumer computes an answer rather than reconstructing the source; models guide *selection*, semantic facts justify *correctness*; binary search is correct on any sorted array and needs no uniform prior, and "a data structure is a posterior" was a metaphor (C28). Rate–distortion and coding analogies become theorems only with specific source, observation, and error models.

| Structure | What it encodes |
|---|---|
| binary search | search over positions in a sorted array |
| interpolation search | a prior that keys are roughly uniform → O(log log n) |
| learned index | a fitted CDF |
| hash table | a random code; universal hashing is "I don't know the source, so randomize against it" |
| Bloom filter | lossy membership with a chosen false-positive distortion; today the author sets it |
| skip list | a randomized balanced tree |
| "sort here for performance there" | amortization under an expected query/insert ratio; interesting orders across operators |
| splay tree | within a constant of the optimal static tree for any distribution without knowing it; dynamic optimality is open |

Approximate internal structures can serve exact interfaces (a filter rejects definite misses and forwards possible matches to exact storage); randomized structure does not imply approximate answers. An unexpected write/query ratio invalidates a *prediction* and the representation stays correct; binary-search code published against unsorted data is a *correctness* failure. Joint optimization can find one ordering that benefits several consumers. The fifty-year lineage of automatic selection ([Systems](../evidence/systems.md)) failed for five reasons the design addresses: no compile budget for search (amortized via content addressing), no workload model in the language (profiles as derived state), no hardware cost model (self-description), no declarative specification in mainstream code (contracts and relations as the store's content), no invalidation on drift (frontier-tagged decisions).

**Every probabilistic lowering carries a worst-case bound** beside its expected cost: tails are where latency-critical code and adversaries live (hash flooding is why Python and Rust randomize hashes; a learned index is attackable the same way). Learning-augmented algorithms give the discipline (near-optimal when the prediction is right, a worst-case bound when wrong; introsort is the folk version); operation-count bounds do not give end-to-end latency bounds; cost-carrying types are the proof mode for the bound and expected-cost analysis for the expectation. Open: joint choice across consumers, live migration, drift detection, adversarial inputs, escaping references, memory peaks, explaining the chosen representation (Q50). Tested by [E1](../experiments/adaptive-collection.md).

## What the engines teach

The heart of a JavaScript engine is inline caches, not tiers; deoptimization reconstructs interpreter frames from optimized ones and is the hardest engineering in the system; a function that deopts too often is marked unoptimizable, the engine admitting its price model was wrong. Most of that engineering recovers information the language destroyed (shape stability, numeric types, property order); inherent is only the compile-latency/quality tradeoff and guards wherever information arrives late. Statically typed Wasm still uses speculative inlining with deopt (C36). What the design keeps: the price model made explicit and plural; the speculation dial handed to authors per region rather than held by the language implementer; tier policy as a controller with hysteresis; deopt as the state-translation field of a transition; the whole as derived state.

**The mapper has its own tiering ladder.** Spatial lowering is placement and routing, which takes hours on an FPGA and minutes in the compilers for statically scheduled accelerators, so install-time lowering on a phone needs a ladder of its own: sequencer interpretation, then a coarse map onto a coarse-grained fabric in milliseconds to seconds, then full spatial placement and routing, with deoptimization between rungs. Daniel's interpret-while-debugging-then-tier-up requires this ladder for the mapper, not only for the program, and the granularity of the coarse fabric is defined by what can be mapped on-device in the time budget (Q66). Open: Q10–Q14, Q48, Q50.
