# Irreducibles: arbitration, progress, and what remains

After every treaty is removed, these remain. Design minimizes them and names where they occur; it never pretends they are free. Each statement is conditional on a model; merging them into one theorem is the error [Positions](../record/positions.md) records (C07, C08).

## Monotone needs no arbitration; non-monotone does

Monotone computation needs no arbitration for its result, at any scale from gates to datacenters; it still contends for finite capacity, which is a progress obligation rather than a coordination one (C76). Two related results, not one theorem (C07): Kahn (a network of continuous stream functions is deterministic regardless of timing or scheduling) and CALM (a monotone logic program is eventually consistent without coordination). Neither bounds memory or latency: a deterministic Kahn network can deadlock with finite buffers, so capacity, backpressure, cancellation, and progress obligations must be first-class beside data dependencies or the abstraction hides exactly what the design came to expose. Arbitration-free: CRDT merges, capability attenuation, append-only grants, monotone aggregation. Arbitration: consensus, revocation, mutexes, uniqueness. The article's conclusions (strict serializability only for non-commutative operations; CRDTs need no authority; shard along lines of rare interaction; escrow tricks) are "minimize arbitration points". The arbitration count of a realization is therefore a published metric beside its energy, and a realization with none is a Kahn network ([Runtime](runtime.md)). A time-slot table is not zero arbitration: slotting moves arbitration to schedule time and requires a shared time base ([Timing and analog](../hardware/timing-and-analog.md)).

**Name the order and the query.** Deriving an attenuated capability adds a weaker token to the set held; "does some token I hold permit this" is monotone in that set, so attenuation is coordination-free, and attenuation does not revoke the parent. Restricting a policy from A to A ∩ R shrinks the permitted set; "is this permitted" is anti-monotone, so cached decisions and issued grants must be invalidated, which is revocation, which is arbitration whose coordination depends on placement, freshness, leases, and already-issued rights (C19). Adding grants can be monotone. Revocation is to capabilities what consensus is to replicas and a mutex is to threads. The claim that all narrowing (attenuation, restriction, refinement, commitment) is uniformly coordination-free is corrected (C18).

## Progress needs capabilities

An empty network does not mean a computation is finished: a producer may still hold the right to emit. Timely dataflow tracks messages *and* capabilities to produce at future timestamps, plus summaries of how timestamps advance through operators. A router computing minima over packets cannot infer that (C16); hardware progress accounting needs a protocol over producers' outstanding rights. A capability to emit at *t* is literally a capability (authority over time), and a frontier is "no outstanding authority to emit below *t*".

## Finite resources

Data dependencies say what may be computed; resource and progress contracts say whether it can continue under finite capacity. Model buffer capacity, credits, reservation, cancellation, fairness, and recovery; a backpressure path can itself deadlock if held resources form a cycle. Little's law relates concurrency, throughput, and latency under its conditions and is not a scheduler. Progress tracking can be summarized hierarchically or eliminated for proven static schedules; per-datum timestamps, global barriers, and one shared frontier are not mandatory ([Change](change.md)). Open: a common liveness and resource contract checkable across circuits, accelerators, streams, and services without importing the most expensive protocol everywhere (Q51); the producer–router protocol and where hardware progress accounting pays (Q24).
